Frequently asked questions

Practical answers about submitting, presenting and attending. Anything not covered here, please ask.

Who is this conference for?

Anyone whose work sits between medieval Hebrew manuscripts and computation: computer scientists building recognition, segmentation or analysis methods; paleographers and codicologists; philologists and textual critics; librarians and curators of digitized collections. You do not need to be part of the MiDRASH project to attend or to present.

What exactly do I submit?

An abstract of 500 words maximum, including the bibliography. Send it to the conference address together with your name, affiliation and the title of your contribution. Full papers are submitted later, for the proceedings volume.

When are the deadlines?

The abstract and full-paper deadlines are To be announced. They will be posted on the Call for Papers page and announced through the project channels.

Do I really have to make a poster?

Yes. The conference runs as poster sessions with short presentations and demos, and all participants are expected to produce a poster. It is a deliberate choice: posters keep the detail of the work available for discussion across both days, rather than passing by in a fifteen-minute slot.

Can I present work in progress, or something that did not work?

Very much so. Evaluations of computational results by humanists are of particular interest, as are cases in which a human expert surpasses the capabilities of artificial intelligence — and cases where the reverse happens. Negative and partial results are welcome if the analysis is careful.

What about demos?

Demonstrations run alongside the poster sessions and are warmly encouraged. If you have a tool, a model, an annotation environment or an interface, bring it and run it. Mention the demo in your abstract so that space and power can be arranged.

In what language is the conference held?

English.

Will there be proceedings?

Yes. The conference proceedings will be published in Electronic Notes in Theoretical and Applied Informatics and Computer Science (ENTICS). Full papers are due after the conference abstracts are accepted; the deadline is To be announced.

Is there a registration fee?

Registration details, including any fee, are To be announced. A registration system will open on this site closer to the date.

Can I attend without presenting?

Attendance arrangements for non-presenting participants are To be announced. If you would like to come as a listener, write to the organisers and we will keep you posted.

Is remote or hybrid participation possible?

To be announced. The format is built around being in the room with the posters, but we will say clearly what remote options exist once they are settled.

What size should my poster be?

Poster dimensions and printing arrangements are To be announced and will be sent to all accepted participants well before the conference.

Is there travel funding?

To be announced. If travel cost is the difference between coming and not coming, say so when you submit — we would rather know early.

Where do the manuscript images on this site come from?

From the Cairo Genizah Collection at Cambridge University Library, made available through the Cambridge Digital Library under a CC BY-NC 3.0 licence. Individual shelfmarks are credited beside each image.

Still have a question?

The organisers would rather answer it than have you guess.
